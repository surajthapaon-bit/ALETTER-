THE LETTER

Date: September 2026.

Recipient: For Stephanie.

Signature: — Suraj.

---

Section 1 — The opening.

Stephanie —

I don't know how to start this except to say: I loved you. I love you.

Not a version of you. Not the idea of you. Not the person I hoped you would become. You.

The one who brought me food when I was working on cars. The one who called me Kancha. The one whose family became part of mine. The one who saw me at my lowest and didn't run.

That person is real to me. That person still matters.

I've been writing to you in my head for seven months. I've written to you in my journal. I've written to you in a whole archive that has kept me sane through everything. But I've never written to you like this. Completely. Honestly. With nothing hidden and nothing held back.

So here is that.

---

Section 2 — The truth about what I did.

I know I hurt you. I know I cornered you. I know I pressured you.

I didn't even know what those words meant until recently. English is not my first language. I've been learning it my whole adult life. So some of how I communicated — the bluntness, the asking for clarity over and over, the urgency — I didn't realize how heavy it felt to you.

When I finally understood what it meant to corner someone, I told you I was sorry. I meant it. I still mean it. And I've been proving it the only way it can be proven — by changing. By learning. By trying not to be the man who makes you feel trapped.

I also know I overexplained. I pushed for conversations at times you weren't ready. I tried to hold the whole emotional system together by myself, and in doing that, I probably made things heavier for you.

That's mine. I own it. I'm still working on it. And I'm not putting it on you.

---

Section 3 — What I see.

Seven months ago, something broke between us.

Not a fight. Not a rough patch. A rupture. Something happened that neither of us ever truly talked about. Not all the way. Not to the point where we could both put it down and move forward.

Instead, we did something else. We kept coming close. You reached for me. I reached back. There were warm moments. There were calls. There were soft messages.

But the rupture stayed on the floor between us. Neither of us picked it up. And slowly, the warmth started to feel like it was standing on top of something unspoken.

That's what I mean by the fog. Not the distance. The closeness that isn't built on the truth.

I can't live in that fog anymore. Not because I stopped loving you. Because living in it is making both of us smaller.

---

Section 4 — What I need.

I'm not asking you to be perfect. I never was. Nobody is. I'm not asking you to have all the answers. I'm not asking you to fix everything overnight. I'm not asking for the version of you that lives in your imagination.

I'm asking for one thing. Consistency.

I need to see the same person tomorrow that I saw today. I need the person who reaches for me to also be the person who can sit in the hard thing with me. I need the warm version and the honest version to be the same person. Not one or the other.

Because here's what I learned over these seven months: I can't live in warmth alone. Warmth without repair isn't a relationship. It's a truce. And a truce requires me to pretend the rupture didn't happen. I can't do that anymore. Not without losing myself.

I've been learning what it means to not abandon myself. That's the whole thing I've been writing about. That's the archive. That's the reason I'm still standing. I can't abandon it now — not even for you. Not even for love.

So what I need is this.

I need us to sit down, face to face, and look at the rupture together. Not to blame. Not to relitigate. Not to decide who's right. Just to put it on the table and look at it honestly, the way two people do when they actually want to move forward.

I need to hear your side, in your words. I need you to hear mine, in mine. I need us to name what happened, not because it's comfortable, but because avoiding it has already cost us seven months.

And after that, I need to see if we can actually be consistent with each other. If we can be the same people on Tuesday that we were on Monday. If we can hold a line without disappearing. If we can stay in the room when the room is uncomfortable.

That's it. That's the whole ask.

I'm not asking for perfection. I'm asking for the floor. And if the floor isn't there, then there's no relationship to stand in.

---

Section 5 — What I'm not going to do.

I'm not going to chase you.

I'm not going to keep asking the same question in different ways. I'm not going to keep filling the silence. I'm not going to keep softening my truth so you'll stay close. I'm not going to keep pretending the rupture didn't happen so we can have the warm version back.

I did that for a while. I understand why I did it. But I can't do it anymore.

Not because I stopped loving you. Because I stopped being able to live with myself while I did it.

I'm also not going to disappear with anger. I'm not going to close the door and lock it. I'm not going to punish you for being who you are.

I'm just going to stop living in the fog. And I'm going to leave the door open for reality.

Not the doorway. The door. There's a difference. I'm not standing in the doorway anymore, waiting. But if you walk toward reality with me, I'll be here.

---

Section 6 — The truth about us.

I don't know if we survive this. I really don't. I'm not writing this to force an outcome. I'm writing this because I needed to say it, and I needed you to hear it, and I needed to say it in a way that was finally mine.

What I do know is this.

You are not the villain. I'm not the hero. We're two people who met at a time when we both needed something, and we gave each other something real. For three and a half years, you were the closest thing to home I had ever known. That's real. That doesn't disappear because of a rupture.

But the version of us that existed before the rupture is gone. It broke. And what we've been trying to do since then is get back to a version that doesn't exist anymore. That's why we're stuck. We're trying to go back instead of forward.

We can't go back. We can only go forward. Together, in reality. Or separately, in peace.

Both are real options. Both are honest endings. But the one thing we can't do is stay in the fog. That's the ending that costs us both the most.

---

Section 7 — Last.

Stephanie.

I want you to know something, and I want you to hear it without any condition attached.

I don't think you're a bad person. I don't think you're cruel. I don't think you set out to hurt me.

I think you're someone who has been carrying your own weight for a long time, in a small world, with not enough people around you, and not enough language for what you've been feeling. I think you did the best you could with what you had. And I think a lot of what broke between us wasn't malice. It was capacity. Yours and mine.

That doesn't make it hurt less. But it makes it human.

And I don't want to write this as a punishment. I want to write this as a door.

The door is still open. But it only opens onto reality. Not the fantasy. Not the fog. Not the warm version that requires me to forget what happened.

Reality.

If you want to walk through it with me, I'll be here.

If you can't, I'll grieve what we had, and I'll keep walking. Not because I stopped loving you. Because I finally started loving myself enough to stop living in the fog.

Either way — I hope you find your way back to yourself. I hope you find the language you never learned. I hope you find the people who can meet you in the small world, and I hope one day you meet yourself in the big one.

And I hope, whatever happens, you know that what we had was real.

That's all. That's the whole letter.

With everything I have,

— Suraj.
